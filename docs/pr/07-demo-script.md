# The 5-minute demo

*The story we tell on stage, beat by beat, re-timed to the story spine, plus the demo script with fallbacks. Part of the pitch docs; start at [README.md](README.md).*

This is what we say and show in the 5-minute slot. The Q&A is [08-qa.md](08-qa.md); the opening options are [hook.md](hook.md). Status is as of commit `6b1cc85`.

Two rules shape it:

- **Every claim stays true.** Each on-screen moment runs in the app, or is said out loud as planned. This version has **one** "planned" moment (studio accounts and creator games, spoken in beat 3), down from three "Coming" frames worth 95 s in the earlier version. Synthetic people are called demo people when it matters (Add friend goes to a bot row).
- **Every beat names who it brings closer** (the top priority since the judge's note on Nov 12).

The rubric sets the time split: AI Integration 30%, Product Thinking and User Impact 25%, Execution and Demo 25%, Originality and Cohesion 20%.

## The script, on the story spine (300 s)

Seven beats in Karthik's order: 25 + 40 + 20 + 20 + 80 + 70 + 45 = 300 s. Muse is visibly working for about 120 s of it (the build runs on the mirrored phone during beats 2 to 4, then beat 6), and the live demo runs throughout.

**The trick that makes the timing work.** A game made from scratch takes a median 99 s (D30). So the presenter types the sentence in the first seconds, agrees "How the game works" (4 to 7 s), and taps Make by about 0:20. The build runs on the mirrored screen while we tell the why, and we come back to it at 3:05.

**Notes on the script.**

- **If the build isn't ready at 3:05,** narrate the lanes: they are the show. If it fails, Studio offers "make it with a ready formula" (median 28 s); start that and play a pre-made real Muse game meanwhile.
- **The judge moment in beat 6** is the strongest AI evidence we have on stage. Keep it to one change. A microgame revision can take over a minute; start it and move on.
- **Say "demo person" once.** Add friend in beat 5 goes to a bot row. Saying so pre-empts the hardest Q&A question.
- **Say the bounds big and true.** "If you can describe a 2D game, Muse can write it" holds for the format (9 of 10 hard ideas, D32), and the seven engine gaps are the roadmap. Don't say "we've built every kind of game": calm and puzzle games are blocked by the gate today unless they can be lost (D32).
- **Market the collaboration.** Every beat from 4 on shows people doing something together: making (beat 4), competing and meeting (beat 5), changing a game with a judge (beat 6).
- **Numbers to say:** 70% (simulated), 12 of 12, 88% (Muse-judged), 99 s (or "a minute and a half"). Not "about a minute", not "30 seconds" for a game made from scratch.

### Prepare two more games (Karthik's bus notes)

- **A gyro-controlled game.** Karthik's note: "Have a game that can be gyro controlled?" Not possible today: device sensors are a named engine gap (`device_sensors`), and the format has no tilt or gyro input. It needs engine work: an input source for device motion in the Rust engine, the runtime bridge passing CoreMotion readings to the WebView, and replays that record them so scores stay verifiable. **Ask for Artem** (engine owner); logged in [open-items.md](open-items.md#asks-from-karthiks-bus-notes). Until then, a drag-to-steer game shows the same idea, and the anatomy card will mark tilt as "New to Muse", which is itself a good demo moment.
- **An intellectual game, in the Harry Potter theme.** Karthik's note, verbatim: "Definitely need to prepare an intellectual game (Krillion for Harry Potter?)". He clarified: he means **Krillion's rare-answer mechanic**. Krillion ([krillion.io](https://krillion.io), July 2026 [134]) is a daily trivia game with seven open prompts: you type one answer in 25 s, and rarer accepted answers score more, in tiers from 10 to 100 points. He wants a Harry Potter-themed version (real franchise names are allowed for requested themes). We copy the mechanic only (open answers, rarer scores more), not Krillion's name, art or tier names.
  1. **"Rarest Spell" (the main candidate; planned).** Open prompts in the wizarding world ("Name a spell that unlocks a door"); type one answer in 25 s; rarer accepted answers score more. Planned as part of Karthik's restructure of the harness. The plan: answers are matched fuzzily, close to semantically: Muse writes each prompt's accepted answers and their aliases (misspellings, nicknames, near-synonyms) at build time, and play time uses deterministic matching against that list, so replays stay verifiable. The rarity tiers are written into the game, since a one-device demo has no crowd data. Give it a run timer so today's gate (which requires a losable run) lets it through.
  2. **"Sort the Sorting"** (backup; Connections-style grouping): 16 tiles (spells, creatures, potions, house traits) into 4 groups of 4, 4 mistakes allowed. Grouping puzzles were in the audit's 9 of 10. Only the share is text, not a grid (the `share_grid` gap). Not built.
  3. **"Who Cast It?"** (backup; a deduction grid): 4 students, 4 spells, 4 classrooms, and 6 clues; fill the logic grid within 3 wrong guesses or 2 minutes. Grid logic puzzles were also in the 9 of 10. Not built.

  Before the demo, make "Rarest Spell" (and one backup) through Studio and keep the logged runs, so every "made with Muse" claim holds.
