1 · The target

Optimizing clicks fills the feed with slop. Optimizing grab and satisfaction doesn't.

The same greedy ranker with the same posteriors. The only change is what it optimizes: clicks (CTR, the usual engagement target) or our reward, which splits grab (did it pull you in) from satisfaction (did it pay off). Week 2.

Clicks only (CTR)Our reward

Bait-averse people's joy per page doubles (0.143 → 0.286), and slop drops from about 42% of pages to about 6% for every kind of person. YouTube and Meta split engagement from satisfaction for the same reason (RANKING.md, section 3).

The honest exception: a plain ε-greedy bandit on each game's mean value scores higher for hook-lovers and balanced people. It already uses our grab × satisfaction value, and it shows hooky games to everyone; figure 3 shows what that costs.

3 · Why

It learns who likes hooks. ε-greedy and click-chasing show the same hooky feed to everyone.

Share of pages that are hooky games (hook strength 0.6 or more), by kind of person. Week 1 and 2.

Formula (ours)ε-greedy banditClicks only

The per-person bait lean is what does this: 60% hooky games for hook-lovers, 19% for bait-averse people. That's where the formula's longer sessions for bait-averse people come from (8.2 pages, against 7.2 for ε-greedy). It's also the most personal feed tested: 21–23% of pages come from each person's favorite kind of game, against 14–19% for ε-greedy.

4 · The on-device test

Interleaving: the formula wins 70% of sessions against today's feed.

Team-draft interleaving, the test Netflix and Airbnb run before an A/B test. Each session mixes the formula's picks with a control's; a satisfying page (a finish, replay, save or social moment) earns its side a point. Share of decided sessions, with 95% intervals. 3,780 sessions per control.

The rows below the first compare the formula with itself minus one part. Interleaving can't see longer sessions (where the bait lean pays off) or the value of exploring, since both sides learn from the same pages. So the bait lean and Thompson rows are judged in figures 3 and 5 too.

5 · Why these parts

What each part adds to joy per page.

The full formula minus the formula with that part switched off. Week 2. Blue helps; red hurts.

Cold start helps everyone the most. Calibration and the reason prior help a little. The bait lean is a deliberate trade: +0.024 for bait-averse people, −0.024 for balanced people. Thompson sampling costs payoff in this simulator, because 90 people share evidence on each game; it's kept for thinner real catalogs, and listed as an open decision.

6 · Why satisfaction counts 2.5× grab

Weighting satisfaction 1.5 against grab 0.6 halves slop, at almost no cost to hook-lovers.

The same ranker with our weights or with equal weights. Calibration, the bait lean and Thompson are switched off, to isolate the weights. Week 2.

1.5 / 0.6 (ours)Equal weights

Equal weights more than double slop for every kind of person and cost bait-averse people 0.044 joy per page; hook-lovers gain only 0.008. Hooky pages for bait-averse people also rise from 62% to 82%.

7 · The guards

The guards hold, and the tests catch it when one is switched off.

Property tests on every page of every session (about 20,000 pages, 2 worlds). Share of pages or windows where the property holds.

As shippedThat guard switched off

The slop property holds even with the bait guard off: the satisfaction posterior alone keeps likely slop out of the top 3. The price is the untested-game cap: a new game waits a median of 51 pages for its first showing, against 20 with the cap off, though every new game is shown. A minimum number of first showings per new game is an open decision.

8 · The optional Muse term (off for the demo)

Capping Ember at ±0.6 bounds it both ways.

Stand-in Embers re-rank the formula's top 10, judged by interleaving against the formula alone. Share of decided sessions, with 95% intervals.

Even an Ember that knew everyone's true taste would add little, and one that ordered worst-first does limited harm. Raising the weight would need on-device wins against the formula alone first.

9 · Group Blend

A Blend lifts the worst-off member's enjoyment from 0.31 to 0.49.

240 synthetic Groups of 4–6 people, 10 games each, scored with members' true enjoyment.

Every blend rule far beats random games. Ours (geometric mean, a misery floor at 0.12, plus-only fairness) is best on the worst-off member and on games everyone enjoys (32.0%), but only by a margin inside the noise (±0.006). Masthoff's group-viewing study also favors the multiplicative rule.

What these tests don't show

  • That the formula beats every standard. A plain ε-greedy bandit on mean value gets more joy per page for hook-lovers (0.515 vs 0.411) and balanced people (0.427 vs 0.323). It uses our grab × satisfaction value, and it shows hooky games to everyone.
  • Anything about real users. The people, the games and the payoff are synthetic, and the simulator rewards grab and satisfaction equally, while the formula deliberately doesn't. On-device interleaving with real people is the test that settles it, and it's built.
  • That calibration's variety matters to people: the simulator has no boredom.
